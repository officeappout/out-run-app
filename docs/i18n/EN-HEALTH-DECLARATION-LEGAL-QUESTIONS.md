# Questions for the owner's lawyer: English health declaration

> Status: **DRAFT.** Context: [EN-HEALTH-DECLARATION-DRAFT.md](./EN-HEALTH-DECLARATION-DRAFT.md). The Hebrew declaration is the Israeli gym-form questionnaire (2015 regulations) plus an added liability sentence. We are about to offer the same flow in English (Israel-based English speakers, a German pilot with English-speaking staff). We do not want to add or remove any commitment without your advice.

1. **Which law governs the English-language user?** Israeli law for everyone who signs in the app, or does the user's country (UK, EU/Germany, US state) change what the declaration and waiver mean? Should the terms say so?
2. **Waiver sentence** ("I alone am responsible for any injury or harm to my health that happens during the activity"). It is not in the official Israeli form. Is it enforceable in Israel as a consumer? Should we keep it, narrow it, or replace it with a separate, itemised assumption of risk? Does a blanket exclusion for negligence risk being an unfair term (Israeli standard-contract law; EU/UK consumer rules)?
3. **PDF template clause naming "the municipality and anyone acting on its behalf"** (apparently a municipal leftover; the Hebrew text is also garbled: `בא אני עוסק באת שימוש`). Who should be released, if anyone: OUT, the local authority customer, both, neither? Please supply the intended Hebrew.
4. **"Declare" without "under penalty".** Does "I declare" create any statutory-declaration or false-statement exposure in English-speaking jurisdictions? Confirm we should not add "under penalty of perjury" or similar, and that omitting it matches the Hebrew.
5. **Health data consent.** Is one checkbox ("I declare ... and agree to the Terms of Use and the Privacy Policy") enough in Israel (Privacy Protection Law, health data as sensitive)? For EU/UK users, do we need a separate explicit consent for special-category data (GDPR art. 9) and a lawful basis note?
6. **Minors.** The official Israeli form needs a parent's written consent under 18. Should the English flow (a) block under 18, (b) add an age confirmation, or (c) add parental consent? What age applies outside Israel (13 for data, 16 in parts of the EU, 18 for contracts)?
7. **Hard stop after any "Yes".** The app blocks registration; the official form allows continuing with a doctor's certificate. In English markets, is a hard stop a discrimination or accessibility risk (e.g. pregnancy, disability), and should we offer "a doctor has approved" instead?
8. **One-tap "all answers are No".** Is the fast-track button (declares all 13 answers at once, answers stored as No) acceptable evidence of a read declaration? The PDF pre-prints "No" on every line.
9. **Which text is the legal record?** The on-screen text and the PDF template differ (two-year renewal, voluntariness, municipality release, "past and present"). Should they be identical? In English, which one should the user see before signing?
10. **Language of the signed text.** We plan to store the language and a version of the text the user saw. If an English user disputes a Hebrew record, or a Hebrew-speaking user signs the English text, which version prevails? Should the terms include a "Hebrew version prevails" clause?
11. **Claims in copy.** "Stored privately and securely and used only to personalize your plan" and "healthy" (fast-track note), "safe workout", "100%": are these representations we must be able to back up? Preferred softer wording?
12. **Retention and renewal.** The official form requires a new declaration after two years and gyms keep records two years. Do these apply to an app? How long do we keep the signed PDF and signature, and should existing Hebrew records be re-papered if the legal text changes?
